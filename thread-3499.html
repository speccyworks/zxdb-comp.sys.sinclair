ARE YOU A TRUE SPECTRUM USER?
 - THE COMP.SYS.SINCLAIR INITIATION CEREMONY!

Here it is again, for those of you who missed it the first time,
and for those of you who liked it so much you wanted more.  It
is now almost twice as big as before.  Shall we called this V1.1?

This is basically a cross/jumble between a proper test, to see
if you can get as many right as possible and impress your
friends, and an excuse to have a bit of a joke.  I cannot claim
that all of the following is my own work any more, unlike the
original, since I have added some of the bits that were posted
to the newsgroup in reply to the original.  However, if you find
something funny, it is probably mine ;-).  Please email me with
your thoughts.

----------------------------------------------------------------

Are you a true Spectrum User?  In order to prove that you lived
through the golden age of computing, you must answer the
following questions correctly!  Only then are you worthy to post
to comp.sys.sinclair! ;-)

1 - You will know what colours the numbers 0-7 correspond to.
0 - Black, 1 - Dark Blue, 2 - Red, 3 - Magenta (NOT purple!),
4 - Green, 5 - Cyan (NOT light blue), 6 - Yellow, 7 - White.

2 - You will know at least one Spectrum game from the following
authors:
Matthew Smith - To obvious to go into.
Julian Gollop - Chaos, Rebelstar x3, Laser Squad
Mike Singleton - Lords of Midnight, Doomdarks Revenge...
Demonic Robinson - Zynaps, Uridium (Spec)
Jon Ritman - Head over Heals, Matchday...
William Tang - Horrace Goes Skiing, Horace and the Spiders...
Keith Burkhill - Commando, Ghosts & Goblins...
Bo Janborg - Fairlight, The Artist...

3 - You will know how to delete on a rubber-key spectrum.
Caps shift and 0.

4 - You can name at least four types of Sinclair Spectrums (none
after Amstrad).  Forget those Timex ones.
Spectrum 16k, Spectrum 48k, Spectrum+, Spectrum 128.  What kind
of idiot would put ZX-81 eh? (Gulp).

5 - You always remember that it is SIR Clive Sinclair.
Although many people also call him Uncle Clive.

6 - You can remember games from the following publishers:
Bug Byte - Manic Miner
Software Projects - Manic Miner, Jet Set Willy...
Ultimate - Sabre Wulf, Knightlore, ...
New Generation - Trashman...
Melbourne House - The Hobbit, Fighting Warrior...
Psion - The Horace games, Survival, Make a Chip...
Artic - Artic Football...
Elite - Commando, Ghosts and Goblin...
Imagine - Arcadia, Stonkers, Alchemist...
Mikrogen - Skool Daze...
Durrell - Saboteur, Turbo Esprit...

7 - You know which game(s) the following characters starred in:
Trashman - Trashman, Travel with Trashman
Monty Mole - Monty Mole, Monty on the Run...
Sabre Man - Sabre Wulf, Underwurlde...
Miner Willy - Jet Set Willy 1 & 2, Manic Miner.
Magic Knight - Finders Keepers, Spellbound...
Ziggy - The Pyramid...
Jetman - JetPac, Lunar Jetman...

8 - You know the dual functions that the Space Bar had.
Space and Break.

9 - You can spell UNDERWURLDE and NONTERRAQUOUS correctly. 
(You'll have to check Nonter.)

10 - You know at least one poke for JSW
Err....  I'm not worthy.  But I do know the WRITETYPER cheat.

11 - You can't stand the keyboards on the Spectrum+ and Spectrum
128.  And the keys keep dropping out.

12 - You know what Mastertronic "MAD" stands for.
Mastertronic Added Dimension.  (I must confess I'm not certain
on that one.)  You got a separate game on side 2.  Guaranteed
only to be played once.

13 - You can make the sound that the Spectrum makes when it is
loading.
Beeeeeep Bep.  Beeeeeeeep bfgkjhfgkhfdgfgkljsgkjg....
Miss out the third Beeeeeeep when impersonating speedlock.

Another test is to recount all the places (except for a room with
a Spectrum in it) where you have heard it: Star Trek - The Voyage
Home?  No, actually, but close enough.

14 - You will know every Spectrum Game ever written.
(Of course I could list them all but it would take up too much
space.)

15 - You will know which number keys corresponded to the cursor
keys.
5 - Left, 6 - down, 7 - up, 8 - right, although even more
important is to actually be adept at using them.  A rare skill
in itself.

16 - You have got an old 48k Spectrum in your attic covered in
3 inches of dust, all the writing on the keys has been rubbed
off, and at least one key doesn't work, probably the one with
LOAD on it.

17 - You prefer to use the keyboard rather than a joystick for
arcade games.  Except for the Ultimate key layout.

18 - You can name at least 4 dedicated Spectrum magazines.
Sinclair User, Crash, Your Sinclair, Sinclair Programs, Your
Spectrum.  Wasn't there a ZX something?

19 - You know NEVER to plug a peripheral into the edge connector
when the computer is turned on!

20 - You know how to select the extended keywords.
Caps-Shift and Symbol shift, and maybe then cap-shift again
depending on your mood.

21 - This was your first basic program:
     10 PRINT "hello hello hello"
     20 GOTO 10
     And you were dead impressed with it.

22 - You remember the following companies:
Dk'Tronics, Cheetah (SpecDrum, Ratt), MGT.

23 - You know that if you type in a program from a book or
magazine, it will never actually work, but you still try it
anyway out of curiosity.

24 - You always say "enter" instead of "return".

25 - You know the two pieces of music that play during the title
screen and during the game of Manic Miner.
"The Blue Danube", and (I think) "If I was a Rich Man".

26 - You know the difference between a K cursor, L cursor, G and
E.  Were there any more?
K - Keyword, L - Anything after K, G - Graphics, E - Extended
mode.

27 - You develop loads of superstitions when trying to load a
game successfully.  These include not leaving the room, walking
on tip toes around the computer, trying to get the tape recorder
as far from the TV as possible to reduce any interference,
disconnecting the one lead (ear or mic?) that you didn't need to
use when loading, and even turning the TV off while loading (and
dreading it when you turned it back on again).

28 - You can remember the following other computers:
Jupiter Ace, Oric, Lynx, Sord M5.  And you liked to laugh as the
companies went bust as the Spectrum got better and better (until
1986, cough...)

29 - Your Spectrum software collection includes at least two
C90's full of pirated games.  Come on!  We all did it!  And those
Ocean games were a pain weren't they (I seem to remember)?

30 - You remember the Horizons tape that came with a Spectrum. 
A game of Thro' the Wall anyone?

31 - You know where the name "Namtir" in Namtir Raiders came
from.
Namtir -> Ritman (backwards).  Jon Ritman wrote it, a common
poster to this group.

32 - You know the four colours making up the stripe on the
bottom-right corner of a rubber-key.
Red, blue, yellow and green.  I think.

33 - You wonder what a Quirkafleeg is, having missed the posts
in the newsgroup regarding it.

And the obligatory:
34 - You play the free Rebelstar Pbem! (etc... places still
free!)

Well that was good.  Here I am asking other people to prove they
are true Spectrum users and I probably got half of them wrong
myself.  Oh well, hope you liked it.

OK let's start from the beginning...

On a +3 or +2A, go to BASIC mode and type the following:

copy randomize

and press enter.  Since it's a syntax error, you would expect the red cursor
to come on, and it does.  However, there is about a quarter of a second
delay between you pressing enter and it displaying the red cursor.  Your
aim is to try and press the keys C, J and L at the same time during that
quarter of a second.  If you do then you will see a friendly message.

There has been a claim that on newer machines you can control the message by
pressing P, L, Enter, 0 and Q, but this certainly doesn't work on my +3.

No, I think the main difference is the floating-point maths. :-)

The idea behind the spectrum's way of drawing arcs and circles is to draw
an arc in several straight lines where the end of each line is calculated
from its start by applying a rotation transformation.  So, apart from the
fact that there's a huge amount of calculation involved in setting up this
transformation, each step consists of taking (x,y) to (v*x+h,w*y+k) where
v, w, h and k are floating-point constants and then drawing a line between
them.
