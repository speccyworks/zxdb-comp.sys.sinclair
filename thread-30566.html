Hi,

As promised, here's an early Xmas present. It's available from the
newly revised Sinclair Basic pages on WoS at:

http://www.worldofspectrum.org/sinclairbasic/

There are instructions and stuff on-line but the main item of interest
is probably this new command:

   SOUND r1,d1[;r2,d2...]

Gives you AY access from BASIC. Works the same as the Timex T/S 2000
BASIC command. e.g.: you give it a string of register/data pairs, and
then do a PAUSE (returning to the editor automatically shuts the sound
off). Examples of how to use it can be found in the Timex 2068 User
Guide available from here:

http://www.timexsinclair.org/

Enjoy!

P.S. bug reports to the usual email thanks.

P.P.S. This is also the first public release with working EDIT, DELETE
and commands.

I read a review of this in last week's Micro-mart (9-15 Dec 2004).
It mentioned that you had "researched Spectrum BASIC extremely 
thoroughly, correcting every single bug".

I too have found the BASIC of interest and have been writing about it 
for twenty years. In 1986 I had a letter about "the secret passageway"
printed in Popular Computing Weekly and the previous year I had a letter 
published that explained the true nature of location 23681.

Just a simple letter I thought and hardly expected that it would elevate 
me to the status of Galileo. I wasn't saying that the earth revolved 
around the sun or was I?

David Lawrence, who owned Popular, had given me a copy of his book for 
an earlier longer letter that was rejected so, feeling guilty, I wrote a 
shorter snappier letter that made it to print in September.  He'd 
suggested that I contact Sinclair Research but I had already done that 
and received a cock-and bull reply. A programmer would later explain 
that Clive does not understand software. Neither it seems does Nigel.

I was not the first to point out that 23681 was the high byte of PR-CC 
but I was the first to contact Sinclair Research. I was the first to 
explain its true nature and that it was not OK to carry on as before.

Surprisingly the magazine continued to publish stuff from other 
programmers that said the sun revolved around the earth. I wrote 
corrections.

When Amstrad printed the same mistake, I wrote to them but was told that 
the sun revolved around the earth. Hundreds of ZX Spectrum books 
repeated this claim. I was the only person to think the earth revolved 
around the sun.

When I started the zx82.txt file, I made sure that each quirk was 
documented and drew attention to location 23681. I have written to every 
one who displays an online, or embedded, manual advising of the need for 
correction.

The Sinclair Extended BASIC source however turns the clock right back to 
1982 and says the following -

P_POSN  equ     COORDS + 2      ; (IY+45)  23679  1
PR_CC   equ     P_POSN + 1      ; (IY+46)  23680  1
USER    equ     PR_CC + 1       ; (IY+47)  23681  1

Furthermore, in a masterpiece of cut-and-paste I find that my statement 
to the contrary appears in the very same document.

There is no hair inside my hands nor scars upon my face and yet I fear I 
may be burnt at the stake for stating that there are no system variables
in the 48K ZX Spectrum that are not used by the ROM.

It would seem that I know more about what is under the hood of even SE 
BASIC than you do.
