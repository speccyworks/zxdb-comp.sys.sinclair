Of course, Sinclair Spectrum nerds all used GENS/MONS (aka. "HiSoft Devpac"
[1]) back then, modded their Spectrums to have at least 4164 RAM chips (or
even 41256) instead of 4116 and either replaced the wire jumper with a
switch or had software-enabled bank switching [2].

After a quick CLEAR 32767, there were >=2 32kB "banks" in the upper half of
the address space and you could host the assembler/source code in bank 1
and the machine code in bank 2. I normally used the screen buffer for
swapping over when there wasn't enough space ontop of BASIC. No need to
save to tape or microdrive between test sessions, just reset, CLEAR,
switch back, ready to go again.

No in-circuit-debugging, no single stepping, of course. Bonus points for
IF-1 development [3], where you not even had the character printing option
of the main ROM.

Cheers,
Volker
