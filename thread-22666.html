2001-07-26 12:22:31 Ole Martin Halck CSSCGC Crap Games Contest 2001 ZXDB: Wild West Head Hunter
Having decided to consider taking part in this year's edition of the CSSCGC,
I webbed my way to http://www.foxbox.org/adam/code/csscgc2001/index.html to
see exactly what "crap" was supposed to mean. The "Defining Crapness"
section gave me the answer -- but also a niggling feeling that a couple of
aspects of crapness are missing, aspects that may be able to 1) lower the
otherwise already appalling standards even further, 2) single out the truly
crap from the poseurs, and 3) increase my chance of a high placing.

Firstly, in the same way that e.g. a game with bad or over-simplified
graphics can still be a triumph when it comes to game-play (think Tetris,
good text adventures, etc), a game where real care is taken to make some
parts good (whether it be graphics, AI or whatever) can still easily be (as
the homepage puts it) "undefinably crap". Surely, a complete failure made up
of some excellent (or at least conscientiously built) parts is even less
impressive than common, run-of-the-mill uselessness? In short, what I'm
talking about is *painstaking attention to the wrong details* or *attaining
a simple, unattractive effect by lots of work*.

An example: Imagine if Tomaz Cedilnik's "Wild West Head Hunter" (admittedly
terrible even now) had actually included code *for the remaining eight
levels as well*! Wouldn't it then have broken new barriers in crapness?
Another example: My eagerly-awaited "CCCC"[1] -- out soon -- stretches the
memory and processing power of the poor 48K Speccy to its utmost due to
concepts, algorithms and techniques *never before attempted on the
machine* -- and I daresay it won't be good. Oh no.

Secondly, on a somewhat related note: Our fond memories of bona-fide
crapness from the eighties are partly due to the aspect of crapness I shall
dub "completely failing to live up to expectations". This aspect induces --
besides the normal frustration, irritation and incredulity -- a
*disappointment* which may, in extreme cases, lead to severe depression. The
reason for this reaction is, of course, *hype* -- a facet I feel should be
reflected in the competition (see previous paragraph and earlier post).
Would "Terror-Daktil 4D" have been as crap if it had been titled
"Terror-Daktil sort-of-3D"? I think not. Would the crapness of <insert any
delayed game here> have shone so brightly if it had been released on time,
rather than being promised "out soon" in full-colour, full-page magazines
month after month[2]? Hardly. Isn't the hidden Excel 97 flight simulator
actually rather good, if only since you don't expect it do exist at all?

There -- that's off my chest. I fully expect the judge to completely ignore
all of this, though. Cos he's probably crap.

[1] No, none of the C's stands for "crap".
[2] Actually, I estimate the probability of my game ever being completed at
about 30 %.

--
Regards,

Ole Martin Halck
