In the case of novel games, I would agree. In the case of MM/JSW sequels, it's
a bit different. Those who play MM/JSW sequels should be no mean MM/JSW players
already, having hopefully mastered the original MM/JSW. So it's fair to throw
them in at the deep end, in the sequels. I consider the first room in J4 to be
a medium challenge by my standards - a good warm-up for me, but not a
head-basher.

I like writing rock hard MM/JSW games, as a challenge to myself and other
MM/JSW experts. Manic Miner 4 has some very difficult rooms after a couple of
really easy ones to start with. The opening room of We Pretty (my next JSW
game) should be enough to weed off the stragglers! :->

In the case of Manic Miner: The Hobbit and Jet Set Willy: Lord of the Rings,
though, I'm writing for a Tolkien audience as well as a MM/JSW audience. I
think I will do two versions of these games: the `real' version, and a
watered-down version for Tolkien fans and those who like their MM/JSW games
easy.

I call it We Pretty because I love esoteric titles! :-)

The specific reason why it's called We Pretty is that I was listening to David
Bowie being interviewed by Mark Radcliffe on Radio 1 FM just before his amazing
Outside album was released (in September 1995). I had not seen a track listing
for the album, five songs from which were played on that show. One of them was
"We Prick You", but I misheard and thought it was "We Pretty"! I thought this
mondegreen was a lovely enigmatic title, so I decided to do a JSW game of that
name!

David Bowie's Outside album is the single biggest inspiration for We Pretty
(which refracts an eclectic mix of diverse inspirations from music, tennis,
dreams and personal experiences). Like Outside, We Pretty is based on a
nonlinear, fragmentary storyline, written up in the form of a diary. Like
Outside, the central theme is art-ritual murder. And like Outside, We Pretty is
intended to be the first of a series.

The concepts underlying We Pretty are very surreal and incompletely specified,
leading the audience to their own personal interpretations. The plot is that the
player has been found guilty of a crime known as abstract behaviour in the 21st
century and, by way of sentence, is deported back to the 17th century (through a
black hole). The player ends up in a parallel universe (the inevitable
consequence of any /backward/ time travel). In this parallel universe, the
player has a doppelganger called Kari Krisnikova - the central character, who
performs the art-ritual murders described in the diary.

The name We Pretty is open to personal interpretation - my own interpretation
concerns the struggle for dominance between forces of good and evil. My idea is
that Kari Krisnikova would appear very fair and innocent to those who didn't
know her (hence Pretty), but is, in fact, a killer cold as steel, as robotic as
a guardian in JSW. Because Kari is the player's doppelganger, they both look
alike (hence We) except that the player is white and Kari is cyan (a colour
symbolism which came to me in a dream).

I know I've been talking about We Pretty for over a year now, but the good news
is that implementation is now seriously underway after I was held back for ages
(due to other commitments) with only up-in-the-sky ideas. I'm now aiming for a
July 1999 release. And up in the sky is the first place we'll be going...

It's interesting that you should mention doing a soundtrack for We Pretty, because lately
I've been thinking about doing just that. I have in mind a 90-minute compilation of music
that has a `We Pretty' atmosphere - music that I associate with the concepts behind the game.
This soundtrack wouldn't actually be released, of course (it would only exist as a private
recording on a 90-minute audio cassette), but I might list the tracks I use in the
documentation in case anyone wants to reproduce the soundtrack!

For the soundtrack, I'll be looking to include pieces of music that reflect Kari Krisnikova's
character - basically an ice-cold, blood-lustful murderess, concealed beneath an innocent,
saccharine exterior. So I want to combine tracks that express musically one or the other side
of this character.

My ideas for the We Pretty soundtrack at the moment are:
- Many David Bowie songs (mostly from Outside, perhaps a few from Earthling, and even some
from Contamination if that comes out before We Pretty!);
- Trent Reznor's "Driver Down" from the Lost Highway soundtrack and Scott Walker's "The
Cockfighter" for their frenzied, chaotic atmosphere;
- An Abba song (namely "Angel Eyes") for the saccharine factor;
- Some Bjork Gudmundsdottir songs (e.g. "Joga", "Bachelorette", "Enjoy" or "Army Of Me");
- Miscellaneous songs with covert We Pretty associations (e.g. Fluke's "Atom Bomb" or
Radiohead's "Electioneering").


> Can you put text in JSW rooms?

The obvious answers are to put text in the room names, and to define the block graphics as
text, or build giant letters from blocks (as in DIE MORTAL in "Entrance to Hades"). The
original JSW engine does not support arbitrary text in the screen layouts, but this could be
achieved using the Patch Vector in Geoff Mode to print text on certain screens as an ad hoc
special effect.

We Pretty will be pretty much based on the code from the original JSW (except
maybe a few patches to disable the colour code and WRITETYPER, and perhaps an
anti-cheat protection mechanism), but these do not affect the actual game play
and shouldn't therefore upset JSW-PC. The big thing that probably /will/ upset
JSW-PC, though, is that We Pretty uses fall-without-dying (POKE 36477,1) - a
little fix to JSW-PC would be needed to detect this in the snapshot and adjust
accordingly, if it doesn't already.

Some of my future games may use more major extensions such as John Elliott's
teleporter overlay and Geoff Mode, thus requiring bigger changes to JSW-PC.

There are going to be loads of mondegreens in We Pretty, mainly from
David Bowie songs. For example, there's this line in "Stay",

    I really meant to so bad this time

which, the way he sings it, I always thought was:

    I really meant to sow my tears to hang

This mondegreen actually sounds better than the true lyric - it's more
typical of David Bowie's impressive-sounding nonsense! :-) So I have
written a room in We Pretty based on the surreal concept suggested by
this mondegreen.

Another mondegreen room title in We Pretty is "The Starry Lasso", from
the song "Volare", the lyrics of which are in Italian. I don't know
Italian, so this line in the song sounds to me like "Fair lee-jay, dee
starry lasso". And there's another room called "gen Italien" ;->
(which translates, I believe, to "to Italy")
