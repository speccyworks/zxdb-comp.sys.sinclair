# The archive

The pages of this site were made from an archive of the whole group: **231,309 posts in 38,372 threads**, from the first post in December 1993 to 30 August 2026. No single copy of the group has all of it, so the archive puts five of them together. Each post is in it once; when several copies have a post, the one with the fullest headers is used. Spam is kept in the archive but left out of the threads shown on the site, and the site shows only the threads its pages cite.

## Dates

All dates are in UTC. usenetarchives.com stores its times shifted by the New York offset (a post sent at 11:06 UTC shows as 07:06, and a post known only to the day shows as 19:00 or 20:00 on the day before); the archive adds the offset back. 46,249 posts are known to the day only: 41,970 that only usenetarchives.com has, and 4,279 Giganews posts from Aug–Dec 2000, whose date has no time (525 more got their time from usenetarchives.com). 6 posts have no date at all.

## What is missing

- **1993–1999, outside Nov 1994 – Mar 1996:** usenetarchives.com is the only copy, and it misses posts: for 1995 the NVG spool nearly doubles the count. Google Groups has these years but cannot be copied.
- **After 30 August 2026:** not included.
- **Threads:** a thread whose subject changed, or a reply without references, may show up as a thread of its own.
- **Headers:** the 63,887 posts that only usenetarchives.com has (28% of the archive) have headers rebuilt from its few fields: the sender is a name only or an address cut short (`richar...@mkbbs…`), and 41,970 of them are dated to the day only. The Eweka copies have no real `Path` header (`not-for-mail`).
- **Texts:** usenetarchives.com sometimes drops uuencoded files or cuts off the end of a post. About 1 in 70 of its posts is shortened this way, so about 900 of the posts only it has are probably not complete.

## Looking for old copies

The weakest part of the archive is the 1990s. Apart from Nov 1994 – Mar 1996, all posts from 1993 to July 2000 come from usenetarchives.com only. That copy misses posts (for 1995, the NVG spool nearly doubled the count), has no real headers and often cuts e-mail addresses short. Google Groups has these years, but it doesn't show the headers and can't be copied.

If you have any of the following, it could fill these gaps:

- a news server spool, or a dump of one, holding comp.sys.sinclair from the 1990s;
- posts saved from a newsreader (for example as mbox, or as the newsreader's own folders);
- mail from the `sincnews` mailing list, which sent the group out by e-mail in the 1990s;
- digests or collections of the group's posts, like the ones on the NVG ftp site.

Even a few months help, especially 1993–1994 and April 1996 – July 2000. Please [open an issue](https://github.com/speccyworks/zxdb-comp.sys.sinclair/issues) in this repository.

## Standards for Usenet messages and archives

**Message format.** There is one standard, revised over the years:

- RFC 850 (1983), then **RFC 1036** (1987): the article format of the 1990s posts; it allows two-digit years and `addr (Name)`.
- **"Son-of-1036"** (a 1994 draft, published in 2010 as RFC 1849): the working standard of the 1990s and 2000s.
- **RFC 5536** (2009, Netnews Article Format): the current standard, built on RFC 5322 (Internet Message Format). Required headers: `From`, `Date`, `Newsgroups`, `Subject`, `Message-ID`, `Path`; it adds `Injection-Date` and `Injection-Info`. **RFC 5537** covers how articles pass between servers, **RFC 3977** is NNTP, and MIME (RFC 2045 on) covers character sets and attachments.
- The **Message-ID** is the only identifier that is the same everywhere, and `References` is the standard way to rebuild threads. Article numbers and Google's thread ids only mean something on one server or service.

**Archive layout.** There is no official standard, only conventions:

- **News spool** ("tradspool", C News and INN): `/var/spool/news/comp/sys/sinclair/<number>`, the dots of the group name as folders, one file per article, numbered by the server (numbers differ between servers). The NVG copy is one. INN also has other storage methods (CNFS, timehash, timecaf) and separate overview index files.
- **mbox** (described in RFC 4155, `application/mbox`): the usual export format, as on archive.org. Its variants do not agree with each other: mboxo, **mboxrd** (every `>*From ` line in a text gets one more `>`, so it can be undone exactly), mboxcl and mboxcl2. Archives often use their own separator lines (see Giganews and the 2020 backup above).
- **`#! rnews <bytes>` batches:** how servers passed articles to each other.
- MH and Maildir (one file per message), borrowed from e-mail, are also used.
