Hi Bethany,

Nice job on the FAQ.  I like the sleeker, simpler version.  I
was reading the Timex reference section, learned a thing or two,
and spotted a couple of small errors:

At one point, talking about port $ff, it's mentioned that
it "seems likely to be partially decoded" as with the Spectrum's
ports.  All i/o ports are fully decoded in the TS2068 (and
presumably the TC2068/TC2048).

The joystick fire button is attached to bit 7, not bit 4
as shown.

The EXROM back is not fully decoded.  This means the
EXROM memory that sits in the 0-8k chunk also appears
in every 8k chunk in the 64k space of the EXROM
bank.

Anyway great job to you and Andrew.

> Fred Meunier tested this on his real TC2048 for Fuse and discovered that
> it's fully decoded in the lower 8 bits only.

Ah, okay.  This is what I call fully decoded.  While
it's true that i/o space is 16-bits wide, the
instruction set leans toward an 8-bit space and so
do Zilog's docs.

Whenever I do hardware, I think of the peripheral's id as
taking up the bottom 8 bits and any further information
required optionally sits in the the top 8.  The keyboard
works this way: bottom 8 bits = 254 identifies the fact
you're reading from the keyboard and the top 8 bits
identifies which scan row you're looking at.

It has been. It was one of the few sections to be completely dropped with
this revision, and it's unlikely to make a comeback any time soon.

Following a 'straw-poll' (last year) of users, there were a few sections
that were considered weak, almost universally. In some cases, this was
because there was generally little enthusiasm for them, or that they were
felt to be continually outdated; so-much-so that people rarely relied on
them and automatically went elsewhere instead (e.g. to RaWW, WoS, etc.)
since they tended to be more regularly notified of changes, and were
generally maintained more often. The most obvious example that comes to mind
was emulators, but there were several others.

That said, there's absolutely no reason not to add this information in at
some point; some of the company sites are already on the 'pending' list for
the links section, and some are already in there. If enough people are
interested, and are willing to help verify/update the previous entries, then
it can be added in quite easily; the old version of the FAQ is still
available from the 'versions' page, so you can use that in the interim, and
can send any verified information that you'd like included to the FAQ
mailbox to be added later.

Following on from that, there's plenty of room for additional content, and
there's a lot coming along - you should expect to see a revision every 4-6
weeks, although not all of them will include a vast amount more information,
there should be a fairly good increase over the last one, and the
'moving-target' sections (links, emulators, etc.) should generally be more
'tight' over time. Note, though, that adding something to the FAQ for the
sake of adding it isn't going to happen; it has to be something that there
is general support for - if you look carefully at the 'new' items in the
FAQ, you'll see that they're quite commonly asked about in css
(documentation, etc.), are frequently emulated (e.g.. peripherals), or are
things that naturally 'belong' but have not been in previous revisions
(Timex, etc.) There is a fair amount of lateral expansion still to take
place in specific places, and if people stop reading certain bits (or didn't
ever start) these will be moved out in favour of more detail about the bits
that are being used.

CSS drives the FAQ; if you want something added, talk about it (I *am*
watching you). Whether I want it or not is unimportant; I just happen to be
the one that types it up. Equally, every entry takes time to research,
verify, write and maintain, so it's only likely to get in there if it's
genuinely going to be used by people. If it's looking 50:50, I have no
problem tossing it out for discussion and going with public opinion. It's
your FAQ, not mine ;)


Bethany
