1999-10-03 20:02:12 …@… Re: Remakes
On Sun, 3 Oct 1999 19:30:03 +0100, Stuart Brady 
wrote:

>IMHO, it just seems a bit silly for people to write software for free,
>any then keep the source. But I'm not complaining -- closed source
>remakes are _way_ better than no remakes at all. :-)

Not silly at all. Consider this: I write Head Over Heels 2000 for DOS (but
runnable from Windows). It gets released, and it get's roughly the same
reaction as KO99 got (i.e. HUGE, and deservedly so). I then release the source
code. Someone takes the source code, improves it no end, adding extra features
and the like, releases it, and suddenly all the praise for HoH2000 goes to
this new guy who didn't neccessarily do much more to it. Recognition of my
work becomes only a side issue, and when MY new version comes out, perhaps
extending that, the response might be "been there, done that". I certainly
wouldn't like that to happen to me, and certainly wouldn't encourage me to
work on any more stuff.

But then that's just me (and a lot of others) - I know for sure that people
think the direct opposite and believe in "the general good" rather than
getting all the credit.

There's also the risk of open scrutiny of your code and it being tactlessly
commented about by complete strangers. Would _you_ want to be called a crap
coder based on a single program that did what it did rather well and after
putting a few hundred hours into it?

>Even though I don't care much for operating systems other than Linux,
>it's nice to see cross-platform programs being written, such as AbiWord
>and Netscape. I like to think that you should be able to run all
>software, regardless of the operating system that you're using.

Which is why all the games I have planned are either based around Java or VRML
- if you have the right viewer and a powerful enough machine, then it doesn't
matter what the O/S is, and no porting is needed. And I certainly WON'T
release my Java code - if you want a new feature, _ask_ me for it to be
included in the next version, if there is to be one. Otherwise, write your
own. Selfish? Yes, but it keeps me in control of all my own work, and any
botch-ups done by other people won't be attributed to me.

>But would anyone port the code? -- Why don't you release it and see?

There's also the possible ego-crushing blow of nobody _wanting_ to port the
program.

Feel free to _ask_ for open sources, though. Just never _demand_ them. ISTR
that you once posted here that you NEEDED a Linux port of KO99. Nobody NEEDS a
port of any program. You made it sound as if it was a matter of life and
death. Get some perspective - it's just a game, albeit a very good one.

recompile.
