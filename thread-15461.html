While you're all waiting for the deliberations of Alistair and I to be
concluded I thought I'd post this little message so that those who
entered the CSSCGC 1999 could see who's likely to win and who's not.
When it comes down to it, the ball is in Alistair's court, so even if
I've said that your game won't win here, there's a good chance that it
probably might possibly (by a long stretch of the imagination) have a
slight hope of perhaps being lucky enough to be fleetingly considered
as a winner. :)

I'd like to congratulate all the entrants, though. The standard as ever
has been pretty abysmal, but there are definitely a few stand-out games
that take unplayability to a new level. Well done.

So, without further ado, here's some brief appraisals of all the items
of evidence. Ahem. I mean entries... (Please note, that although I may
be very cruel about the game you wrote, it's probably for the best and
you'd be advised to give up programming and take up a profession that's
less likely to find you writing the software for some critical system
in a nuclear submarine's launch subroutines)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Aberdeen - This game loses out as it provides a valuable insight
 into why no-one should ever visit the God-forsaken titular city.

A Day In The Life Of Mike The Mole - This is a fantastically
 playable game that relies upon split-second reactions and the
 the RND statement not conspiring to block your exit and make the
 game impossible to play. Far too enjoyable, but it does have a
 very nice error in the shape of a "3 subscript wrong" error when
 you start the game and press LEFT. Crap, but not crap enough.

Advanced Basic Debugger - Plain crap, and obviously trying to cash
 in on the controversial success of Derek Jolly's winner from last
 year. I will not tolerate this!

Advance Coin Sim - Unfortunately it does accurately emulate the random
 nature of a coin falling and therefor is far too good to win.

Advanced Pro Wife Simulator - The rather nice looking machine code
 screen fade for this game made it look far too professional, and the
 software emulation of a wife's AI was much too realistic for this to
 be considered anything but a triumph of programming design.

Arcade Emulator - This is an excellent piece of software that
 provides all the thrills and spills of finding out that your
 favourite arcade game is now pushing up the daisies because of
 it's corrupted ROM. However it isn't really a game, is it? Tsk!

Arklay Football Club - I've never liked football management games
 and so this automatically fulfils the requirement of being crap
 by it's very nature. But it's not going to win, as I completed it
 on my first try and it didn't even force me to sit through a three
 minute rendition of "Here we go!" played on the BEEP chip.

Buffy The Vampire Slayer - This game had all the depth and playability 
 of a normal TV/Movie gaming tie-ins and as such was indistinguishable 
 from some of the best Spectrum licensed games like Knight Rider and 
 Street Hawk. Sorry, far too good.

Class 1 Lawnmower Racing - Although this game had lots of crapness, it
 was all over far too soon, and didn't force me to sit through any
 interminably long BEEP tunes when I won. So, again it lost.

Class 2 Lawnmower Racing - Very similar to it's 1 player precursor, this
 is in with a chance of winning due to it's cleverly exploiting the fact
 that once one player has started, as long as he doesn't release his key
 and can fend off the other player he'll win. Truly crap.

Chemistry Test - This game sent me into a hypnotic state of boredom, and
 as such has a valuable future in the field of hypnotherapy, and is
 therefor anything but crap. However, should it ever be harnessed by
 Paul McKenna in the making of one of his dreadful TV shows, then it may
 be re-assessed.

Clifftop View - As an accurate simulation of a Seagull this game 
 excelled, and indeed is better than many of the titles that regularly 
 top the Spectrum sales charts. It also includes a strategic element in 
 the way it forces the player to balance the pros and cons of flying low 
 to increase accuracy. Far too good to win.

Connect 3 - An excellent attempt to destroy one of my favourite counter
 related games. Unfortunately the AI is far too good for the game to be
 really crap, despite the deeply flawed basic gameplay. 

Curse Of The Sandwitch - This had many elements of crapness that places 
 it high among my possible winners, from massively long beep tunes 
 (which are unfortunately in-tune, which does detract from the game's 
 crapness level) to the fact that it's an adventure game (and I loathe 
 those buggers). This is a game to look out for in the possible winner's 
 pen. If anything can seriously harm this game's chances it the 
 excellent usage of graphics that easily rivals the best that the GAC 
 ever created...

Dodgy's Castle - If there's anything more annoying than a text
 adventure, it's a text adventure that not only doesn't understand
 abbreviations for the cardinal directions of north, south, east
 and west but which also forces you to type in every instruction
 twice. Truly crap on every level, and as of now a excellent bet
 for winning the 1999 CSS CGC.

End Of The World Simulator - The realism of this program had me cowering
 behind the sofa with the same degree of terror that Daleks cause in me,
 meaning that it cannot possibly win because of its authenticity.

English To Giant Translator - This isn't even a game! However
 it is program that's of immense use if you're seriously
 planning that package tour up the beanstalk that's curiously
 appeared in your back garden. Therefor it's not crap. Sorry.

The Enforcer - I would have happily paid Â£7.99 for this game, such is my
 love of it's classic gameplay, and superb machine code music. I suggest
 that the author of this game immediately removes himself to the nearest
 software developer and uses this pinnacle of gaming excellence to get
 himself a job there.

The Enforcer 2 - The amazing 3D visuals of this game made me duck as 
 bullets came screaming out of my monitor and thudded into the wall 
 behind me. A truly worthy follow up to The Enforcer and a game of such 
 excessive excellence that it cannot possibly be considered crap in any 
 way.

The Entertainer - A 128K rendition of the tune that gets just enough of 
 it's notes completely and ear-jarringly wrong to ensure that I proclaim  
 it the crappest program of the competition. Unfortunately it's not a 
 game, so I have to disqualify it. Bad luck that man.

Europevision - This is a good attempt to capture all the crapness of
 the Eurovision Song Contest in gaming form. Unfortunately it's let down
 by the fact that I played it through to completion no less that four
 times and Ireland didn't win once, indicating that the programming 
 behind the voting simulation is deeply flawed. I'm not sure whether 
 that's a good thing or not, though.

Fantastic Adventure - This game boasts some lovely colour-cycling around 
 the edge of the screen and it's this fact that ensures that it can't 
 win because it really looked rather nice. Not even the fact that the 
 game can be won with the single line "OPEN DOOR" can help this game 
 snatch defeat from the jaws of success.

Forgotten Village - Despite being truly scared by the grisly opening 
 loading screen, I ventured deeper into this game to find that it was a 
 worryingly competent looking text adventure with good descriptions and 
 an adequate parser. How this didn't end up on a Crash cover tape I'll 
 never know. The battle sections of the game where also as exciting as 
 any WWF match, and for all these reasons this game cannot be classed 
 as crap, merely misguided.

Hunchback 84 - Is no better or worse that any of the similarly themed 
 games that Ocean released, however it did generate a "B Integer out of 
 range" error message, which is my favourite kind of error message as it 
 shows a true lack of coding talent. This is one to look out for.

Honest Joe - This game could have almost been a license of Lock Stock 
 And Two Smoking Barrels, a film that I immensely enjoyed. Unfortunately 
 for the game, this mental association of mine means that I had nothing 
 but fun in Joe's quest to gain Â£100. Even when I was caught by the 
 rozzers (ouch!).

Little Batty - This lost out simply because I actually had fun playing
 it, and therefor it must be of some merit, a fact that got disqualified
 instantaneously.

Manic Miner Pre-school Edition - A triumph of level design, and a game 
 that almost ensured that Derek Jolly won for a second year running, 
 until careful investigation upon my part revealed that he didn't even 
 do all the coding himself, and that evidently some friend of his called 
 Matthew Smith had collaborated with him in the production of the game. 
 I also decided to disqualify him because the filename for his entry had 
 more than three characters in it.

Mow Business - This is an attempt to update the traditional
 Lawm Mower Sim by including an extra level of complexity and
 therefor boredom to the equation by giving you a choice of
 four garden tools, which you can swap between at any time.
 Within seconds of starting the game I was utterly bored by this
 complete tripe. However it's not going to win in the hope that
 it'll disuade you people from producing more of these bloody
 lawn mower games... Grrr!

Nails - Without even going into the game proper, this game is
 my favourite by the virtue of having a loading screen that
 features "Vince" from the excellent Rex The Runt animation
 series. However, after going into the actual game, I find that
 it confounds me intensely by playing a BEEPed rendition of a
 tune from a cartoon that I can't remember the bloody name of,
 and further shoots itself in the foot by having a graphic
 description of the incredibly painful process of having your
 fingernails removed without anaesthetic.

Nuclear Reactions - This game immediately lost out due to it's stunning
 loading screen, which was far too good and made excellent use of
 colour. It also made out that I caused a nuclear meltdown, and I don't 
 like having that kind of rumour spread about me.

Octopus Light - This game is very similar to the Nintendo Game And Watch
 titles of the early 80's, and as such brought back many happy memories 
 of school. A side-effect of this was that I found myself in too happy a 
 mood to possibly be nasty about this game.

Playing With Polygons - Derek Jolly once again gets far too clever for 
 his own good in providing a program that in no way can be considered a 
 game, and which has only one practical use in that it can be used to 
 generate a fairly authentic reproduction of the final boss of Arkanoid, 
 "Doh". I was also expecting an entertaining game about dead parrots, so 
 this one loses out by being neither a game, and also by breaching the 
 trades and descriptions act.

Pod - Pod is a low-budget version of Little Computer People, and
 although in many ways crap it loses out by the amazing coincidence that 
 it actually understood the first two words I typed in (those being 
 "dance" and "jump") meaning that the programmer was far too thorough in 
 his researching of the human experience and it's digital
 implementation.

Queen Puzzle - A brilliantly conceived puzzle executed to the full
 extent of crapness by it's lack of any challenge. This is a hot
 contender for the winner's podium...

Rock Paper Scissors - This game captures all the excitement of the
 traditional game used to decided who will carry out some particularly
 vile task when a coin isn't available for the tossing. The programming
 is spot on, and the graphics clear and lovely. So it loses on every
 count. The loading picture is also very good, but the author does win
 some points for obliterating part of it with a blacked-out "Program:"
 message.

Really Smart Game - Although it features excellent use of spelling
 mistakes this game loses out by including all the depth and subtlety
 of modern-day Japanese RPG's in it's battle system, while not
 encumbering the player with some tedious story about the fight
 between good and evil or the coming of age of some psychically
 gifted youth. Bad luck, Andy.

Sentences - This seems to be randomly spewed words with little or
 no gameplay, as a result it uncannily mimics my most hated of all
 genres, the text adventure and instantly loses out.

Sheepdog - This game boasts dodgy controls, piss-poor graphics and
 negligible gameplay. In other words, it's currently odds-on favourite
 for the winner unless something else truly crap appears.

Shut It - This games features a full motion video rendition of the
 beginning of the seminal police drama, The Sweeney (shown at
 approximately 1 frame every 20 seconds) and digitised speech. This
 places it on the same level of competence as most of Codemasters
 releases. It also features many excellent graphics, but it cleverly
 avoids including any gameplay, and so could indeed win the competition
 should every other participant die in a horrible kitchen appliance
 related disaster.

Snow - This is a blatant rip-off of an old BBC-B program that was
 used at every opportunity every Christmas to annoy the hell out of
 the teachers. However this isn't Christmas, so this program simply
 served to remind me that the weather is far too hot and that I was
 pining for colder times. Grrr!

Scissors Paper Stone - This is an insipid re-tread of the earlier
 entry Rock Paper Scissors, but without the graphical flair of it's
 superior relation. The sterile presentation and simplistic gameplay
 belies the fact that this probably started out in a book by Tim
 Hartnell.

Think Of A Number - Brilliantly capturing all the tedium associated
 with the statement "Guess who I saw today!" that's often posed by
 annoying people this game only loses because it's by Derek Jolly,
 and I'm damned if he's winning two years in a row. Ha!

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Graham Goring
