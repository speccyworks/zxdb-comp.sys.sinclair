I'll step in here, if I may...

TZX is a standard for preserving tapes in digital form. Until TZX/TAP
format, there was no one rigid method, and most of the WOS archive was in
.z80 or .sna (snapshot) format. These are all well and good, especially for
the older games, but you lost things - the loading screen$, any extra levels
in the game, and of course, the high pitched screech the spectrum made
whilst loading.

TZX stores images of the tapes themselves - it's up to the emulator to
reproduce the pulses at the emulated Ear socket of the emulated spectrum,
and if the emulation is up to scratch, the original loading tones and
effects can be heard and seen (IYSWIM).

Old tapes are rapidly losing their data. The magnetic patterns degrade over
time, and you're left with no game at all, so we need to get the tapes into
TZX format *now* before it's too late. See WOS/Andy Barker's pages for info
on the STP (Spectrum Tape Preservation) project. Also, the TZX-Vault carries
the fruits of those labours, a good few thousand tapes preserved for the
rest of time[1].

If you have a collection of games that are MIA (Missing in action) then you
need a copy of TAPER or MAKETZX, and get converting!

D.

[1] Probably, until "files" are out of data and we use something else. Can
you imagine, in 100+ years, the "TZX Preservation Project"?
