The TZX format, before it reached it's current state, started off too complex
for it's own good - agreed. However, up until the availability of cheap disk
space and the CSW2 format, it was the best we had. CSW2 isn't ideal either, but
I'll come to that.

Z80/SNA etc can't store loaders. It's as simple as that. It may be more ideal in
this case, for the sake of the game, but you'll not find Gauntlet in snap
format - not if you want to play any more than 8 levels per snap. Also, you can
play the [WTSS] game, and that's great. But you'll not see it load, not hear the
sounds of the loader with a nostalgic tear in your eye. You'll not see the nifty
artwork as the loading screen comes up. And TAP is no good for the majority of
commercial games out there, due to the timing restrictions.

As for CSW/CSW2... It's great. It's small - about 3 - 4 times the size of a TZX,
but still small for what you get. But it's a nightmare from an emulator author's
point of view. In a TZX we (the authors) have access to not the *sound* of a
tape, but the BYTES that make up the data coming in. If we want the sound, we
can extrapolate a wave from that data. If we want to ease the pain of 8 minute
loaders, whilst still maintaining the colours of the loader borders, and the
Screen$ artwork, we can - by loading very fast because we can make intelligent
guesses about what's coming in and where to put it. We can stream the bits in at
a much faster rate too, by short-cutting some code like the INC/DEC B loop in
many loaders. We can even flashload a lot of them (but that's where the benefits
end, imo - may as well snap those). With CSW/CSW2 we have access to the sounds
that are coming in. Are they bytes? are they pilot tones? synch pulses? The
emulation doesn't care, it'll load them. But we can't do much acceleration
beyond basic loader traps looking for square wave edges. We can't analyse the
bytes in the file to rip stuff out... Not easily. It's just a WAV file
compressed really.

Myself, I'd defend the TZX format. It only gets as complex as you allow it to.
And there's no point in saying that this z80 is ok, that tzx is not - I know for
a fact that the z80 used by jim at speccy spoilers came from the WTSS tzx on the
vault. What good is any image of a game, tzx or no, if the source was bad in the
first place?

You could load the original tape and still get the ramp bug.

I'd not criticise the TZX Vault team - they "do a bloody rotten job very well
indeed" to paraphrase a famous comedian/author. It's the best we have, and they
can only work with the sources they have. Prior to the STP/SDP team's efforts,
snaps and z80s and hacked TAP files was all we had, and I for one think that
their quality control is pretty good, but more important is their willingness to
re-try tapes that the public find wanting. These guys aren't TOSEC, out to get
all they can - they're out to get the best that they can.

D.

Given the amount of checking that goes on, especially in speedlock routines, I'd
find it very hard to believe that the loader would still function if MakeTZX had
corrupted even 1 of the edges that was coming in. Although not foolproof, and
there will be times where a certain number of bits are corrupt but the same
result is made from the error check (be it an XOR, CRC or whatever) these are so
remote as to be not worth considering, imo. Don't forget that MakeTZX doesn't
actually load the program, it just listens to it and creates a TZX from what it
hears. There *is* some sort of routine which will identify an individual loader,
(which I think is a bloody stupid and severely limiting way to go about it, but
there you go) but this shouldn't produce any errors.

Tbh, I can't see that there will be any difference between a tzx and an original
tape, *when looked at from the POV of the speccy's EAR port*.

the hand. Maybe it's necessary to find and/or find uses for all the other
stuff (like the knife on one of the mountain paths and the globe in the
