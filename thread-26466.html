How many times have the phone codes changed in Britain since I've been
alive? Let's see. First there was when London went from 01, to 071 and
081, then there was when they added a 1 after the zero, so London changed
to 0171 and 0181, then they changed all the codes for major cities
rendering my entire contacts book useless. Cardiff went from 01222 to
something completely unrelated. And then of course they changed all the
mobile numbers for Cellnet customers from 0141 to 01791. And how many
times have they added digits to existing numbers? I remember when people
had three digit telephone numbers following a four digit code in some
places. They're up to six digits now and most people are up to eight. Ok,
Here's my point:

If the x-xxx-xxx-xxxx format works for the whole of north America, why the
hell can't we impiment it in Britain and have done with it? AT&T did a lot
of research before choosing this system and they adopted it because the
longest series of numbers most people can comfortably remember is seven,
and groups of three are easier to remember. So you dial a 1 if you're
dialling outside your area, then a three digit area code (unless you're
dialling inside your area), and then the seven digit number (the first
three digits of which will refer to an even localler exchange). This
format has enough capacity for all the monbiles, faxes, computers, etc in
the whole of north America, so it should be ample for Britain. And what
does BT give us?

variable length area code (so you don't know if you have the right number
of digits)

followed by eight digit number (which cannot be committed to memory by
half the population).

However, I agree that the phone numbering system is rather stupid. 
They've rationalised it a bit, so looking at Andrew's US format string -
UK version:

(xxxx[x])-xxxxxx[xx]

The first digit is a zero if you are dialling outside your local area. 
The second digit identifies what you are dialling (1 and 2 are fixed line
