2010-07-26 09:29:02 Dan Re: Ann: Serial adaptor for the interface 1-bis
Dear Mr. Shmakov,

Thank you so much for taking interest in my posting.

Regarding your first question, I chose the PIC18F4550 because:
1. It comes in a DIP package.  This is absolutely necessary, as I do
ONLY wired prototypes.
2. It has exactly the required capabilities, not less and not more
either.
3. At 12 MIPS, it is just fast enough.
4. And last but not least, I happen to own a Microchip programming
device.
Factors not taken into consideration:
1. Price, because it is irrelevant for hobby project.
2. Availability of ready-made firmware. Firmware provided by
manufacturers has merely a marketing function: to convince the
customers that their chip is working, but is otherwise not suited for
real-life applications, or else it wouldn’t be free. As for other open-
source USB stacks floating around on the internet, they are written
mostly in C, mainly by total beginners and have at best an amusement
value.  By the way, I wouldn’t be caught dead programming a
microcontroller in C.

As for the second question, I didn’t quite understand your reference
to http://www.obdev.at/products/vusb/index.html.
All what is shown there is an, admittedly otherwise amazing, bit-bang
simulation of a USB port. Now, this may be a retro-computing group,
but I wonder if anybody would be THAT old-fashioned as to refuse using
a microcontroller with hardware USB support.
I aimed anyway at something different: one of the possibly shorter,
faster and more robust stacks around, which is perfectly feasible,
because hobbyists have an advantage over professional programmers:
they don’t need to insure their livelihood, and that of their
families, with maintenance, customer support, bug fixes and service
packs till they retire, so they can AFFORD to write proper code, that
works first-time.

Best regards,

Dan

Well, here's my 2 cents (I'm not a hardware guru nor a programmer,
just an end user):

First, keep up the good work. Any hobby requires a lot of time to
bring things "to life", so congrats.

It looks interesting, but the fact that it needs a "different"
Interface 1 turns it into a curiosity for people like me, who are
enthusiastic about these things but can't build them. If you're doing
this just as a hobby that's perfectly fine, but if you would like your
projects to have a user base they would need to 1-appeal to users, 2-
be for sale, 3-have great support from you or from documentation
(online or offline or both).

I've always been a hardware enthusiast. When I think of serial
communication I immediately think of Paul Farrow's work and utilities
to use an original IF1 or the serial port on the original 128K or
later models to send/receive data between a Spectrum and a PC. There
are also adapters and leads that convert the non-standard Sinclair
serial ports to standard 9-pin ones. When I think of USB the ZX Matrix
comes to mind. I haven't bought it yet, but it's fully supported,
documented and uses software that is also used on other IDE
interfaces.
And we now have the Spectranet (still under development, but fully
documented and full of possibilities) and the SIF (also full of
possibilities, but not a lot of English support or documentation for
now).

Again, keep up the good work. I hope my constructive criticism won't
be seen negatively.
